# Visual Direction and Character Consistency

## Character Lock

Document front, side, back, portrait and material details. Fix facial structure, eye color, hair silhouette and parting, headwear, garment cut, accessory shapes, shoes, palette, stylistic treatment and proportions. Do not redesign an established character/product simply to make it "prettier".

Every generated scene should preserve three layers:

- **Locked:** Full character attributes, source references, and prohibited deviations.
- **Variable:** Pose, expression, setting, lens, time, lighting and composition.
- **Output:** Aspect ratio, text-safe area, resolution target, transparency and use case.

Review each generated image against the approved design (face, hair, clothes, accessories, body, fingers, fabrics and colors) and record `PASS / REWORK / NOT_REVIEWED`. A single good front-facing image is not evidence of all views.

## Brand Design Decisions

- Choose a brand feeling (e.g., future × confidence × approachability) and define about 3–5 observable visual rules.
- Define tokens: primary/accent/background colors, display vs. body typography, corner radius, section rhythm, shadows, materials, whitespace, alignment.
- For reference sites, record **principles only**: information structure, typography rhythm, section transitions, motion ideas and whitespace. Preserve source URLs/rights notes; do not replicate whole pages.
- Reserve readable title and CTA space in hero visuals; mobile crops must not cut out faces, product silhouettes or critical copy.

## Deliverable Style Contract

```
Brand / character, purpose, audience
Character lock: fixed vs. flexible traits
Color tokens: background, foreground, primary, accent, warning
Typography and grids: mobile / desktop
Scene table: purpose, focal point, crop, aspect, media status
Prohibitions: unlicensed media, character drift, fictitious results
```
